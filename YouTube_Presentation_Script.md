## SLIDE 2: Project Background (0:30 – 1:30)

**Script:**

"Let me begin with the project background. XYZ Company has successfully developed a new food or drink product that is currently in the testing phase. The product will be launched in the market soon. However, before launching, the company faces a critical problem — they need to understand how consumers will accept this new product. They must compare consumer preference for this new product against similar existing products already available in the market. Without proper market data, the company risks a poor product launch, which could result in significant financial losses.

So what is the solution? Our project proposes conducting a customer acceptance survey. We will target five strategic shopping mall locations across Kuala Lumpur — specifically Jaya Jusco at 1 Utama, AEON Mall Shah Alam, Bangsar Shopping Center, Pavilion KL, and Suria KLCC. Our goal is to collect a minimum of 500 valid survey responses over a 4-month period, using a face-to-face questionnaire method. This approach will give XYZ Company reliable, first-hand consumer feedback to make informed decisions about their product launch."

## SLIDE 3: Project Objectives (1:30 – 2:30)

**Script:**

"The project objectives were developed using the SMART approach. Let me explain each criterion.

First, the objectives are Specific — we will conduct a customer acceptance survey using a structured questionnaire instrument at selected major shopping malls in Kuala Lumpur. This is not a vague goal; it clearly states what we are doing, how we are doing it, and where.

Second, the objectives are Measurable — we aim to collect a minimum of 500 valid survey responses, with at least 100 responses from each of the 5 locations. This gives us a concrete target to measure success against.

Third, the objectives are Achievable — we will utilize established research methodology and a trained survey team, supported by proper planning and coordination.

Fourth, the objectives are Realistic — we will leverage established relationships with shopping mall management to secure access and permissions for conducting the surveys on-site.

Fifth, the objectives are Time-bounded — the entire project will be completed from 1 June 2026 to 28 September 2026, which is approximately 4 months.

Additionally, we set a Budget-bounded constraint — the maximum budget for the entire project is RM 15,000.

The four main objectives are: first, to conduct the customer acceptance survey for XYZ Company's new product; second, to collect feedback from consumers at 5 strategic locations; third, to analyze consumer preferences and acceptance levels; and fourth, to provide actionable recommendations to XYZ Company's management based on our findings."

## SLIDE 4: Project Scope Statement (2:30 – 3:30)

**Script:**

"According to the PMBOK Guide, a project scope statement should be developed using the Scope Checklist, which includes six key components. Let me walk you through each one.

The first component is the Project Objective. Our objective is to conduct a customer acceptance survey at 5 locations in Kuala Lumpur within 4 months, gathering consumer feedback on XYZ Company's new product.

The second component is the Deliverables. These are the specific outputs we must produce: a finalized survey questionnaire, survey data from at least 500 respondents, a data analysis report, the final research report, and a management presentation.

The third component is the Milestones. We have identified key completion points — M1, the Project Kickoff on 8 June; M3, the Questionnaire Completed on 21 July; M6, the Survey Completed on 17 August; and M8, the Report Submitted on 16 September.

The fourth component is the Technical Requirements. We need a questionnaire designed with both open and closed questions, a minimum of 100 responses per location across 5 major shopping malls, and statistical analysis capability using tools like SPSS or Excel.

The fifth component is the Limits and Exclusions. Our project is limited to Kuala Lumpur only, with a budget cap of RM 15,000 and a 4-month duration. We explicitly exclude product modification, distribution analysis, and any online survey component — our survey is face-to-face only.

The sixth component is the Acceptance Criteria. For the project to be considered successful, we must collect a minimum of 500 valid questionnaires, cover all 5 locations, achieve at least a 70% response rate from approached respondents, and receive management approval for the final report."

## SLIDE 5: Work Breakdown Structure (3:30 – 4:30)

**Script:**

"The Work Breakdown Structure, or WBS, is created using the decomposition method as outlined in the PMBOK Guide. Decomposition means breaking down the project into smaller, more manageable components until we reach the lowest level of detail, which we call Work Packages.

Our WBS is structured as follows. The root element is the Customer Acceptance Survey project itself. At Level 1, we have 9 major phases. At Level 2, we have 47 sub-deliverables. And at the lowest level, we have 55 Work Packages, each with defined duration, resource assignments, and cost.

The 9 major phases are: Phase 1, Project Initiation with 4 tasks; Phase 2, Project Planning with 5 tasks; Phase 3, Survey Design with 7 tasks; Phase 4, Location Preparation with 6 tasks; Phase 5, Team Preparation with 5 tasks; Phase 6, Field Survey Execution with 8 tasks; Phase 7, Data Analysis with 6 tasks; Phase 8, Report Writing with 7 tasks; and Phase 9, Presentation and Closure with 7 tasks.

An important point about WBS naming convention — WBS elements use nouns, not verbs. For example, we say 'Survey Questionnaire' rather than 'Develop Questionnaire'. This keeps the WBS consistent and professional."

## SLIDE 6: Gantt Chart Overview (4:30 – 5:30)

**Script:**

"Now let me explain the Gantt chart, which is the primary scheduling tool used in this project. The Gantt chart was created using ProjectLibre, which is an open-source project management software.

A Gantt chart has several key components. First, we have Summary Tasks — these are the bold bars that represent the 9 major phases of the project. Second, we have Sub-tasks — these are the detailed bars for individual activities within each phase. Third, we have Milestones — represented by black diamond symbols with zero duration. Fourth, we have Logical Relationships — arrows connecting dependent tasks that show the sequence of work.

The most common relationship type used in our project is Finish-to-Start, meaning a task must be completed before its successor can begin. Looking at the schedule structure, we have 9 summary tasks representing the phases, 47 individual activities, 9 milestones, and FS relationships linking the tasks together. Where possible, we have also included parallel activities — for example, Phases 3 and 4 run simultaneously to optimize the total project duration.

The project spans from 1 June 2026 to 28 September 2026, which is approximately 4 months or 86 working days."

## SLIDE 10: Network Diagram & Dependencies (8:30 – 9:15)

**Script:**

"The network diagram in our project uses the Precedence Diagramming Method, or PDM, with an Activity-on-Node approach. This means each activity is represented as a node or box, and the arrows between nodes represent dependencies.

There are four types of logical relationships in project management. The most common is Finish-to-Start, where a successor activity starts only after its predecessor finishes. We also have Start-to-Start, where activities can begin in parallel. Finish-to-Finish means both activities finish at the same time. And Start-to-Finish, which is rare, means a successor finishes when its predecessor starts.

In our project, we primarily use Finish-to-Start relationships. We also use the concepts of Lead and Lag. A Lead is a negative time adjustment that allows a successor to start before the predecessor finishes — for example, FS minus 3 days. A Lag is a positive time adjustment that adds waiting time — for example, FS plus 5 days. In our project, we have kept all relationships at FS with zero lag to maintain a clean, straightforward schedule.

We also have Burst Activities and Merge Activities. A Burst Activity is one activity that has multiple successors — for example, Milestone M2 is a burst activity because it leads to both Phase 3 and Phase 4. A Merge Activity is one activity with multiple predecessors — for example, Milestone M5 requires both Phase 3 and Phase 4 to be completed before the team preparation phase can begin."

## SLIDE 11: Quality & Risk Management (9:15 – 9:45)

**Script:**

"Quality management is essential for ensuring our project delivers value. According to PMI, quality is defined as the degree to which a set of inherent characteristics fulfill requirements. Quality brings value to customers.

We implement quality through two approaches. Quality Assurance, or QA, refers to the methods, processes, and activities that produce the desired outcome. For example, we use a structured questionnaire design process and proper survey methodology. Quality Control, or QC, refers to monitoring performance and taking action on results. For example, task 6.6 is a dedicated quality check on collected data, where we review responses for completeness and accuracy before proceeding to analysis.

For risk management, we identified four key risks and developed response strategies for each.

First, low response rates at certain locations — our response strategy is Mitigate. We will offer small incentives and approach more respondents than needed to ensure we meet our 500-response target.

Second, mall permission delays — our response strategy is Avoid. We will contact mall management early in the project and follow up regularly to prevent delays.

Third, weather delays affecting field work — our response strategy is Accept. Since weather is unpredictable, we will accept the delay and reschedule critical activities as needed.

Fourth, budget overrun — our response strategy is Mitigate. We will maintain a contingency budget and reduce material quantities if necessary to stay within the RM 15,000 limit."
